01

Origins

From Nigeria to Queens

Hino was born in Nigeria and moved to the United States in his early teens. LeFrak City, Queens became the setting for the next chapter: school, soccer, New York pressure, and eventually boxing.

The exact birthplace and immigration year differ across published accounts. This preview keeps the public wording broad until Hino confirms the record.

02

The decision to fight

A serious education in pressure

By 2000, Hino had reached the 178-pound Open final of the New York Daily News Golden Gloves. He did not win that final. The distinction matters: the honest version of a career is more powerful than an inflated one.

He turned professional in 2004 and competed at cruiserweight through 2009, compiling a 15–4 record with seven knockouts.

03

The Contender

The last man across the ring

On Season 4 of The Contender, Hino defeated Darnell Wilson, Deon Elam, and Rico Hoye to reach the championship bout. Troy Ross stopped him in the fourth round of the final.

Runner-up is the accurate result. The run placed Hino in front of a wider audience while leaving the harder work — reinvention after competition — still ahead.

04

From boxer to trainer

The ring became a language

After his professional career, Hino moved into coaching and boxing-based conditioning. He created Hollywood Hino Training Camp and built sessions around fundamentals, work capacity, presence, and accountability.

ESPN documented Hino training Carmelo Anthony and the two sparring at a Degree Men event in 2013. Later profiles followed the growth of his fitness and cultural work.
